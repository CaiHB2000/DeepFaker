\begin{abstract}
Detecting multimodal misinformation requires combining textual and visual cues while coping with topic shift and annotation noise. We present Dynamic Modal Priority Distillation (DMPD), a reliability-aware gated distillation framework that routes supervision from a multimodal teacher to a student only when (i) a fused prediction is confident and self-consistent across modalities, and (ii) the post belongs to an event with a good reliability history. The objective emphasizes calibration and positive-class recall via temperature-scaled KL, lightweight feature alignment, and evidential regularization. Unlike standard distillation that copies teacher predictions indiscriminately, our design selectively transfers knowledge across modalities and events, reducing the propagation of confident errors. We evaluate on two Chinese multimodal rumor datasets (Weibo and WeFEND) and a synthetic English benchmark (MiRAGeNews) with strong single- and multimodal baselines, and provide ablations on gating, event reliability, and calibration. Code, configurations, and scripts are released for full reproducibility.
\end{abstract}
