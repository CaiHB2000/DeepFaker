Abstract
The prevalence of fake news, in both visual and textual forms, has
become a critical and urgent issue. Existing multi-modal fake news
detection methods aim to improve detection performance by leveraging information from different modalities and integrating it into
a unified, comprehensive representation. However, challenges arise
when different modalities hold conflicting beliefs. This suggests
the existence of information asymmetry and imbalance between
various modalities, which has not been adequately recognized in
previous work. In this regard, focusing on the uncertainty of each
modality facilitates a more nuanced understanding of its reliability
and informativeness. In this work, we distinguish among uni-modal
information sources through accurate estimation of their decision
uncertainty, employing this uncertainty as a confidence measure
and indicator of information imbalance to guide feature fusion.

Inspired by the prevalent issue of imbalanced information in
multi-modal fake news data, both text and image modalities require
individualized processing based on their unique characteristics and
call for authoritative guidance in the subsequent integration process. To address this challenge, we accurately estimate the decision
uncertainty of each uni-modal information source, employing this
uncertainty as a confidence measure and indicator of information
imbalance to guide subsequent feature fusion. As shown in Fig 2,
different modalities within the same news pair exhibit varying uncertainty levels. Specifically, the red box in the image highlights
deceptive information (e.g., a president paired with a gun), while
the text mistakenly labels the content as "real" due to the lack of
such obvious misinformation. This discrepancy indicates a lower
decision uncertainty in the image modality, making it more likely
to produce confident predictive outcomes. If we were to perform a
simple average fusion, the result might be biased by the text and
lead to a decision contrary to the ground truth. However, by incorporating uncertainty assessment, the image modality is assigned a
lower uncertainty score, meaning its features will be given more
weight during the subsequent fusion stage.
Building on this concept, we present a new framework called
T-FND. T-FND begins by using the outputs of uni-modal detectors
to model the parameters of a Dirichlet distribution, allowing for
accurate uncertainty estimation for each modality. Unlike previous
fusion approaches, T-FND employs these uncertainty estimates to
guide the fusion of uni-modal features. In this process, modalities
with higher uncertainty are down-weighted, while those with lower
uncertainty are given greater emphasis. By accurately assessing
uncertainty and employing uncertainty-based feature fusion, TFND effectively addresses modal imbalances, thereby enhancing
the reliability and accuracy of multi-modal fake news detection.

2.1.3 Uncertainty-based Learning. Uncertainty quantification (UQ)
has become an increasingly important component in developing
trustworthy machine learning systems. It has been successfully applied across various downstream tasks [9, 10, 17, 19, 26]. Accurate
uncertainty estimation is essential for improving the reliability of
predictive models, particularly in high-stakes applications such as
medical diagnosis and, potentially, multi-modal fake news detection.
Recently, evidential deep learning has been introduced as a promising approach, using a single neural network and a single forward
pass to estimate prediction uncertainty at a given point. Uncertainty receives attention as it can serve as a measure of confidence
in each modality and provide a qualitative indicator of information imbalance. In this work, to address the issues of imbalance in
multi-modal data and achieve reliable classification, we leverage
uncertainty at the decision level as guidance for fusing multi-modal
features. When one modality lacks confidence in identifying misinformation, its respective view is down-weighted in the subsequent
fusion process.

2 Related work
2.1 Fake News Detection
Fake news detection is defined as the process of using AI to determine the authenticity of a given social media post and categorize
it as either real or fake. Existing fake news detection methods can
be broadly categorized into two approaches: uni-modal and multimodal. With the growth of social networks, there is a trend for
news to shift from uni-modal to multi-modal formats. Multi-modal
fake news poses greater challenges.
2.1.1 Uni-modal Approach. Both language-based and vision-based
fake news detection have received extensive attention. The study of
text misinformation has undergone significant evolution, transitioning from traditional machine learning methods to more advanced
deep learning models and their combinations [6]. In recent years,
there has been a notable shift towards the adoption of transformers
and pre-trained language models like BERT [4] for misinformation
detection, resulting in improved performance outcomes. Apart from
deepfake [21] studies, the sole visual modality has received relatively less attention. A recent example is the work of MVNN [25],
which innovatively integrates spatial and frequency domain features of news for forensic analysis. However, for the multi-modal
news, these approaches are unable to detect cross-modal correlations.

Proposed Approach

The overview of the proposed T-FND is shown in Fig 3. We design
a dual-stream network: one branch is dedicated to accurately estimating the uncertainty of each modality, while the other focuses
on feature fusion based on this uncertainty to provide the final
classification result. In the uncertainty estimation module, we use
a Dirichlet distribution-based uncertainty learning method, where
higher uncertainty reflects lower confidence in the corresponding
modality. In the feature fusion and decision module, we incorporate
each modality’s uncertainty as a linear guiding factor for fusion,
with modalities of higher uncertainty being down-weighted and
those of lower uncertainty receiving greater emphasis. By considering uncertainty, we can effectively weight each modality’s
contribution during the fusion process, resulting in more accurate
and reliable outcomes.
For each piece of news 𝑥, we leverage a pretrained Vision Transformer [7] and BERT [5] to encode the image and text into unimodal embeddings 𝑥 𝑣 and 𝑥 𝑡 , respectively.

2.1.2 Multi-modal Approach. Recently, there have been notable
advancements in fake news detection methods that leverage crossmodal discriminative patterns to achieve superior performance.
One such method is EANN [29], which incorporates an additional
event discriminator to aid in feature extraction and learn crossmodal characteristics. Another approach, MVAE [15], introduces a
multi-modal variational autoencoder that learns probabilistic latent
variable models and reconstructs the original texts and low-level
image features. Similarly, MCAN [31] utilizes multiple co-attention
layers to effectively fuse textual and visual features for accurate
fake news detection. CMC [30] uses a two-stage network that trains
two uni-modal networks to learn cross-modal correlations by contrastive learning, and then fine-tunes the network for fake news
detection.
Additionally, there are methods that utilize both uni-modal and
multi-modal information. For instance, CAFE [3] measures crossmodal ambiguity by evaluating the Kullback-Leibler (KL) [16] divergence between the distributions of uni-modal features. The learned
ambiguity score is then used to linearly adjust the weights of unimodal and multi-modal features before final classification. However,
CAFE assigns equal weights to each modality, whereas the confidence levels of different modalities should not be the same. Another

Traditional fusion methods, whether through simple concatenation or attention-based mechanisms, tend to treat different modalities equally, without accounting for the uncertainty inherent in
each uni-modal representation. CAFE [3] quantifies cross-modal
ambiguity by assessing the KL divergence between the distributions
of uni-modal features. The resulting ambiguity score is then used to
linearly adjust the weights of uni-modal and multi-modal features
prior to final classification. However, the modality fusion used by
CAFE imposes a constraint that the text and image modalities share
the same weight. When the uncertainty of a modality is high, we
should not rely heavily on information from that modality to make
decisions. Conversely, when the confidences of both modalities are
low, it is necessary to integrate both modalities to make a comprehensive judgment. Based on this, we propose a fusion method
driven by uncertainty.
In T-FND, the input to the final classifier is obtained by adaptively concatenating two sets of embeddings: one set consists of the
uni-modal representations 𝑢 𝑣 and 𝑢 𝑡 , and the other set consists of
the cross-modality correlations 𝑚 𝑓 from the cross-attention fusion
module. This concatenation process is governed by the uncertainty
scores 𝑢 𝑣 and 𝑢 𝑡 provided by the aforementioned uncertainty evaluation module, and it is described as follows:

Implementation Details

4.3.1 Experimental Setups. All our experiments were conducted on
a single NVIDIA V100 GPU using the PyTorch [22] framework. The
image encoder utilized ViT-B/16, while the text encoder employed
BERT-base. The two uni-modal classifiers in the uncertainty estimation module were implemented as multi-layer perceptron (MLP)
layers with a softplus function as the output activation function.
The classifier in the feature fusion module used a fully connected
layer with a softmax activation function. Training employed an
early stopping strategy to prevent overfitting, and learning rate
decay was managed using ReduceLROnPlateau. The experiments
were conducted with a batch size of 32, and AdamW was used
as the optimizer for training over 50 epochs. We used the same
split ratio (7:1:2 for training, validation, and testing) for Weibo,
following EANN [29]. For Twitter, an eighth of both real and fake
training samples form the validation set. Similar to EANN [29] and
BMR [34], text that is either absent or shorter than five words is
replaced with "No text provided", while images smaller than 64×64
pixels are padded with a zero matrix.
4.3.2 Evaluation Metric. The evaluation metrics we used are consistent with those in previous multi-modal fake news detection
works: accuracy, recall, precision, and F1-score.

In this paper, we introduce a new framework called T-FND to address the issue of information imbalance in multi-modal fake news
detection. Unlike previous methods that primarily focus on semantic consistency, T-FND leverages uncertainty scores derived
from a Dirichlet distribution to assess the confidence level of each
modality. These uncertainties are then used to guide the fusion
process of uni-modal features, ensuring more reliable and accurate
classification outcomes. Experimental results on two widely used
datasets, Twitter and Weibo, highlight the importance of considering modality-specific uncertainty to improve multi-modal fake
news detection.
